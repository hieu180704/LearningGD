2. A1 — CÁC GIAI ĐOẠN: IDEATION → PROTOTYPE → PLAYTEST → ITERATE
======================================================================

- Fullerton mở đầu phần quy trình bằng cách gọi thẳng đây là một chuỗi có thứ
  tự: từ ý tưởng ban đầu đến một trải nghiệm chơi được và thỏa mãn.

    QUOTE:
    Having a good solid process for developing an idea from the initial ideation process into a playable and satisfying game experience is another key to thinking like a game designer.
    NGUỒN: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/book-excerpt-game-design-workshop
    NEO: Design Process</h2> (đầu mục "A Playcentric Design Process")
    ĐỘ TIN: CAO

- Fullerton liệt kê tường minh trình tự các bước của "quy trình lặp"
  (iterative process) — đây chính là ranh giới giữa các giai đoạn ideation /
  prototype / playtest / đánh giá, trình bày dưới dạng danh sách gạch đầu
  dòng trong bài gốc:

    QUOTE:
    Player experience goals are set.
    An idea or system is conceived.
    An idea or system is prototyped. (This may mean a physical or digital prototype, or even storyboards or an animatic for an idea that can be presented to players.)
    The idea or system is playtested or shown to potential players for response and feedback.
    Results are evaluated against player experience goals.
    Issues are prioritized and addressed by making changes to the prototype.
    The prototype is retested and the results are evaluated against experience goals again.
    This process is repeated until solutions are found and the design is deemed to meet the goals.
    NGUỒN: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/book-excerpt-game-design-workshop
    NEO: Here is a detailed flow of the iterative process that you should go through when designing a game:
    ĐỘ TIN: CAO

  (Ghi chú: đây là danh sách bullet trong bài gốc, mỗi dòng một ý — đã chép
  đúng thứ tự xuất hiện, không tự sắp xếp lại. Dấu "•" đầu mỗi dòng trong HTML
  gốc đã bỏ để tránh lẫn với gạch đầu dòng của file này, phần chữ giữ nguyên.)

- Dấu hiệu "được phép sang giai đoạn sau" theo Fullerton nằm ngay trong câu
  cuối ở trên: chỉ dừng vòng lặp (tức chỉ được xem là xong một giai đoạn) khi
  "solutions are found and the design is deemed to meet the goals" — nghĩa là
  tiêu chí chuyển giai đoạn không phải thời gian đã bỏ ra, mà là kết quả
  playtest khớp với player experience goals đã đặt ra từ đầu.

- Richard Lemarchand (phỏng vấn) xác nhận trực tiếp cấu trúc bốn giai đoạn của

The book guides people through four phases that guide them, steadily but surely, towards a finished game.
    NGUỒN: https://gamesuserresearch.com/richard-lemarchand-playtesting-and-a-playful-production-process/

    ĐỘ TIN: CAO

3. A2 — PROTOTYPE VẬT LÝ / PROTOTYPE THÔ
======================================================================

- Đoạn giải thích rõ nhất của Fullerton về physical prototype: làm ngay sau
  brainstorm, làm bằng gì, ai chơi, và mục tiêu là gì:

    QUOTE:
    Another key component to playcentric design is that ideas should be prototyped and playtested early. I encourage designers to construct a playable version of their idea immediately after brainstorming ideas. By this I mean a physical prototype of the core game mechanics. A physical prototype can use paper and pen or index cards or even be acted out. It is meant to be played by the designer and her friends. The goal is to play and perfect this simplistic model before a single line of code or art asset is ever made.
    NGUỒN: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/book-excerpt-game-design-workshop
    NEO: Another key component to playcentric design is that ideas should be prototyped and playtested early. (câu trước là hết một đoạn khác — đây gần như là câu mở đoạn, NEO lấy chính câu đầu của đoạn)
    ĐỘ TIN: CAO

- Vì sao phải làm trước khi viết code (hậu quả nếu không làm): Fullerton nói
  rõ hệ quả của việc bỏ qua bước test cơ chế lõi sớm — lỗi thiết kế bị phát
  hiện quá trễ trong quá trình sản xuất:

    QUOTE:
    This way, the game designer receives instant feedback on what players think of the game and can see immediately if they are on track to achieve their player experience goals. This might sound like common sense, but in the industry today, much of the testing of the core game mechanics comes later in the production cycle, which can lead to disappointing results. Because many games are not thoroughly prototyped or tested early on, flaws in the design aren't identified until late in the process—in some cases, too late to fix.
    NGUỒN: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/book-excerpt-game-design-workshop
    NEO: The goal is to play and perfect this simplistic model before a single line of code or art asset is ever made.
    ĐỘ TIN: CAO
    (Ghi chú: bản HTML gốc có một chỗ ngắt dòng lạ "pro-duction" — đã sửa lại
    thành "production" liền vì đây rõ ràng là lỗi tách âm tiết do dàn trang
    web, không phải lỗi chính tả cố ý trong nguồn; không phải trường hợp "lỗi
    có sẵn cần giữ nguyên".)

- Richard Lemarchand (phỏng vấn), về việc prototype thô/vụng vẫn có giá trị
  ngay cả khi chưa ra hình hài game:

    QUOTE:
    I think that no matter how small and clunky, and maybe un-gamelike a prototype that you make is, there's incredible value in putting it in front of somebody else, and just seeing how they react, seeing how they approach it, seeing if they can work out the controls that you haven't properly explained, seeing what they can get just through play.
    NGUỒN: https://gamesuserresearch.com/richard-lemarchand-playtesting-and-a-playful-production-process/
    NEO: (đoạn mở đầu mục "Playtesting early in development" trong bài phỏng vấn)
    ĐỘ TIN: CAO

- Lemarchand — lời khuyên nổi bật (được chính trang trích làm pull-quote):
  lúc prototype nên nhắm tới "món đồ chơi" (toy) chứ chưa phải "game":

    QUOTE:
    I tell my students that when they're prototyping, looking for interesting game verbs that might lead them to a more fully developed game design, it's usually much better to make a toy than it is a game.
    NGUỒN: https://gamesuserresearch.com/richard-lemarchand-playtesting-and-a-playful-production-process/
    NEO: (câu ngay sau đoạn nói về giá trị của việc cho người khác chơi prototype vụng)
    ĐỘ TIN: CAO

- Đồng quan điểm "toy trước, game sau" từ nhóm Experimental Gameplay Project
  (Kyle Gabler, trả lời phỏng vấn về cách họ làm 50 game trong 1 học kỳ, mỗi
  game ≤ 7 ngày, 1 người/game):

    QUOTE:
    Start with the core mechanic. Whether spring systems, swarm behavior, gravity, etc, it never took more than a few hours to get the basic theme up and running. This "toy" should be the core mechanic of the game minus any goals or decisions. There is no win or lose state, just a fun thing to play with.
    NGUỒN: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-to-prototype-a-game-in-under-7-days
    NEO: Mr. Gabler: (đây là câu trả lời của ông cho câu hỏi về quy trình làm prototype)
    ĐỘ TIN: CAO

  (Ghi chú xung đột nhẹ với Fullerton: nhóm này làm prototype trên máy/code
  ngay từ đầu (digital toy, vài giờ dựng xong), không phải prototype giấy như
  Fullerton nhấn mạnh. Hai nguồn không mâu thuẫn về MỤC TIÊU — "toy" đơn giản,
  chưa có thắng/thua — mà khác nhau ở PHƯƠNG TIỆN, tuỳ loại cơ chế. Nên trình

B2. HOÀN THÀNH MỘT GAME

- Derek Yu coi "finishing" (khả năng hoàn thành) là một KỸ NĂNG riêng, luyện được, chứ

  xong việc.

  QUOTE:
  If you treat finishing like a skill, rather than simply a step in the process, you can
  acknowledge not only that it's something you can get better at, but also what habits and
  thought processes get in your way.
  NGUỒN: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game
  NEO: it's probably why they have the recognition I don't!
  ĐỘ TIN: CAO

- Về phần cuối dự án ("90% cuối" tốn công): Derek Yu mô tả đây là giai đoạn chi tiết hoá —
  thứ khiến một hệ thống "chạy được" (competent) biến thành một hệ thống "tốt và không lỗi",
  và việc này có thể tốn nhiều tháng dù phần khung chỉ mất một tuần.

  QUOTE:
  They say the last 10 percent is really 90 percent, and there is truth to this. It's the
  details that end up taking a long time. Sure, maybe you coded a competent combat system
  in a week... but making it great and making it complex (and bug-free)... these things
  can take months.
  NGUỒN: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game
  NEO: 15. THE LAST 10 PERCENT
  ĐỘ TIN: CAO
  Lưu ý: cụm "the last 10 percent is really 90 percent" là câu nói dân gian trong ngành mà
  Derek Yu dẫn lại ("They say..."), không phải do ông tự đặt ra — không gán câu này là phát
  minh của riêng ông khi đưa vào bài học.

- Kỷ luật cắt tính năng để về đích — Derek Yu gọi thẳng là "CUT. IT. OUT.": một khi đã có
  lõi cơ chế được kiểm chứng là vui (core mechanic đã prototype), mọi thứ khác nên bị coi là
  "phần thêm" có thể cắt, thậm chí đang che khuất phần hay nhất của game.

  QUOTE:
  If you've been building upon some core concepts that are provably fun, just keep cutting
  until you get to the very edge of that core. Everything else is probably just fluff you
  could do without. Or worse, it's fluff that's preventing people from seeing the best
  parts of your game.
  NGUỒN: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game
  NEO: forces us to make tight games that feel like they have a purpose.
  ĐỘ TIN: CAO

- Một bẫy tâm lý khác khiến dự án chết giữa chừng: viện cớ "code đang rối, làm lại từ đầu
  sẽ nhanh và tốt hơn". Derek Yu bác bỏ thẳng — cho rằng đây là vòng lặp vô tận, không phải
  giải pháp.

  QUOTE:
  STOP. NO. This is true at some point during every game's development. Your code will
  always be a mess. You will have learned a lot. It will never be perfect. And if you
  start all over, you'll find yourself in the exact same situation when you get to this
  point again. It's a terrible trap to think like this.
  NGUỒN: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game
  NEO: could do it a lot better and faster, and then the rest of the game will go a lot faster, too!
  ĐỘ TIN: CAO

- Bổ sung từ David Wehle (The First Tree, GDC 2019): ví dụ thực tế về việc cắt hạng mục để

- Về việc "actually start" thay vì chuẩn bị mãi: Derek Yu phân biệt rạch ròi giữa CHUẨN BỊ
  để bắt đầu (viết ý tưởng, viết design doc, gom team, làm engine riêng) và THỰC SỰ bắt đầu
  — tiêu chí ông đưa ra là: một "game thật" phải chơi được (playable), nếu chưa có gì chơi
  được thì chưa phải là đã bắt đầu làm game.

  QUOTE:
  Writing your idea down is not starting the damn game. Writing a design document is not
  starting the damn game. Assembling a team is not starting the damn game. Even doing
  graphics or music is not starting the damn game. It's easy to confuse "preparing to
  start the damn game" with "starting the damn game". Just remember: a damn game can be
  played, and if you have not created something that can be played, it's not a damn game!
  NGUỒN: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game
  NEO: 2. ACTUALLY START THE DAMN GAME
  ĐỘ TIN: CAO

- Muốn biến ý tưởng lớn thành một lát cắt chơi được: Derek Yu khuyên tìm ra "core mechanic"
  — cơ chế lõi phải tự nó VUI khi thực hiện, vì đó là hành động người chơi lặp lại nhiều
  nhất — và dùng chính cái lõi vui đó làm điểm neo để quyết định cắt gì (xem quote "CUT IT
  OUT" ở B2). Nói cách khác: lát cắt nhỏ đáng làm là lát cắt chứa được cơ chế lõi đã được
  kiểm chứng vui, không phải lát cắt chứa nhiều nội dung nhất.

  QUOTE:
  Find core mechanics that are just fun to play around with. It should be fun to execute
  the most basic interactions, because that's what players will be doing the most when
  they play your game. Ultimately, you want this core to drive your development. This will
  make it a lot easier for you later on when you have to cut out parts of the game (#13) -
  you'll always have this core to fall back to.
  NGUỒN: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game
  NEO: 5. MAKE SURE THE CORE MECHANICS ARE FUN
  ĐỘ TIN: CAO

- Daniel Cook bổ sung góc nhìn ngược: đừng chọn ý tưởng qua suy luận trên giấy (design doc)
  rồi cam kết ngay, vì phần lớn lựa chọn "có vẻ hợp lý" hoá ra không vui khi làm thật — nên
  cách khả thi hơn là thử nhiều hướng nhỏ, rẻ trước khi chọn hướng để đầu tư công sức lớn.
  (Xem quote đầy đủ ở B1 — cùng một câu, áp dụng được cho cả B1 và B3 vì đây là lý do gốc
  của việc "chọn ý tưởng khả thi" thay vì "ý tưởng hay nhất trên giấy".)

### QA3  [fullerton]
Another key component to playcentric design is that ideas should be prototyped and playtested early. I encourage designers to construct a playable version of their idea immediately after brainstorming ideas. By this I mean a physical prototype of the core game mechanics. A physical prototype can use paper and pen or index cards or even be acted out. It is meant to be played by the designer and her friends. The goal is to play and perfect this simplistic model before a single line of code or art asset is ever made.
SRC: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/book-excerpt-game-design-workshop

### QA4  [fullerton]  [de-artifact: ngat dong giua "pro-duction"]
This way, the game designer receives instant feedback on what players think of the game and can see immediately if they are on track to achieve their player experience goals. This might sound like common sense, but in the industry today, much of the testing of the core game mechanics comes later in the production cycle, which can lead to disappointing results. Because many games are not thoroughly prototyped or tested early on, flaws in the design aren’t identified until late in the process—in some cases, too late to fix.
SRC: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/book-excerpt-game-design-workshop

SRC: https://gamesuserresearch.com/richard-lemarchand-playtesting-and-a-playful-production-process/

### QA11  [proto7]
We found that generally any gameplay idea can be prototyped effectively in less than one week. Extra slop time tends to yield diminishing returns. Some prototypes, for instance, took just a single evening to throw together, while others got an extra week or two of love. Surprisingly, we found that there was no correlation between time spent in development and how successful the game ultimately turned out.
SRC: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-to-prototype-a-game-in-under-7-days

My “Potato” ended up being a perfectly simulated soft body system built in Flash. The only problem was that it was in no way fun. It caused more headaches than death metal, wasted a week, and it didn't even really move. You've got to know when to hold ‘em and know when to fold ‘em.

### QA13  [proto7]

### QB1  [derekyu]
I’ve found that there are three types of games that pique my interest: games I want to make, games I want to have made, and games I’m good at making.
SRC: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game

### QB2  [derekyu]
In my opinion, the ideas with the most potential (to be finished, at least) fall into all three categories and also satisfy the requirement “I have the time and resources to actually make this”.
SRC: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game

### QB3  [derekyu]
Well, that’s great, actually! Because now you’re forced to decide what is really important to your game, and what you could cut. The fact is, if we all had unlimited resources and unlimited time, we’d all make the same crappy, meandering everything game and there’d be no reason to play at all. It’s our limited resources and time that forces us to make tight games that feel like they have a purpose.
SRC: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game

### QB4  [derekyu]
So go back down, down, down, down to a point where you may even find it somewhat beneath you. For example, instead of jumping from your 4x space sim to your 4x space sim IN 3D, try making a great game that focuses on one small element of space sims. And if you can’t finish that, try something more like Asteroids.
SRC: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game

### QB5  [cook]
Even for the most experienced designer, 50% to 80% of your ‘educated’ selections are going to be complete dogs. Every unproven mechanic you polish ends up being a massive drain on your budget and your reputation as a designer.
SRC: https://lostgarden.com/2008/12/06/post-it-note-design-docs/

### QB6  [derekyu]
If you treat finishing like a skill, rather than simply a step in the process, you can acknowledge not only that it’s something you can get better at, but also what habits and thought processes get in your way.
SRC: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game

### QB7  [derekyu]
They say the last 10 percent is really 90 percent, and there is truth to this. It’s the details that end up taking a long time. Sure, maybe you coded a competent combat system in a week… but making it great and making it complex (and bug-free)… these things can take months.
SRC: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game

### QB8  [derekyu]
If you’ve been building upon some core concepts that are provably fun, just keep cutting until you get to the very edge of that core. Everything else is probably just fluff you could do without. Or worse, it’s fluff that’s preventing people from seeing the best parts of your game.
SRC: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game

### QB9  [derekyu]
STOP. NO. This is true at some point during every game’s development. Your code will always be a mess. You will have learned a lot. It will never be perfect. And if you start all over, you’ll find yourself in the exact same situation when you get to this point again. It’s a terrible trap to think like this.
SRC: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game

### QB10  [derekyu]
Writing your idea down is not starting the damn game. Writing a design document is not starting the damn game. Assembling a team is not starting the damn game. Even doing graphics or music is not starting the damn game. It’s easy to confuse “preparing to start the damn game” with “starting the damn game”. Just remember: a damn game can be played, and if you have not created something that can be played, it’s not a damn game!
SRC: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game

### QB11  [derekyu]
Find core mechanics that are just fun to play around with. It should be fun to execute the most basic interactions, because that’s what players will be doing the most when they play your game. Ultimately, you want this core to drive your development. This will make it a lot easier for you later on when you have to cut out parts of the game (#13) - you’ll always have this core to fall back to.
SRC: https://www.tumblr.com/makegames/1136623767/finishing-a-game
